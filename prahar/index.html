Prahar, a wristwatch that keeps the eight watches of the day

A hand-wound wristwatch from Bombay that keeps the day the old way: in eight prahars of three hours each.

Reserve Prahar One

The hands on this page show your own time, and the page takes the colour of the prahar you are in.

Tell

Under the hands, a ring of eight enamel fields turns once a day. The colour that sits beneath the pointer at twelve is the prahar you are in.

Open

It comes apart in the order a watchmaker lifts it: crystal, bezel, hands, dial, then the 188 parts of calibre P·01. Every one of them is drawn live as you scroll.

Wind

Forty turns of the crown pass through two steel wheels and coil the mainspring inside its barrel. It lets go over 72 hours.

Turn

Four wheels gear the barrel's slow pull up, tooth into leaf, until the last one goes round once a minute. They are turning at their true speed here.

Beat

The balance swings six times a second. Each swing lets the escape wheel move on by half a tooth, and that small release is the tick.

Eight watches of the day

Before the hour reached India the day was kept in prahars: four from sunrise to sunset and four through the night, each with ragas meant to be heard in it. Choose one and the whole page turns to its light.

Each painting is a page from one Jaipur ragamala set of about 1750 to 1770, made to picture the mood of its raga.
