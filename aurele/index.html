Méridien

A hand-wound wristwatch with a ring for day and night, finished by hand on both sides.

The hands on this page are set to your own time.

The dial

A ring for day and night

Beneath the hands, a ring of ivory and midnight enamel turns once in twenty-four hours. What rests under the pointer at twelve tells you which half of the day you are in.

Architecture

One hundred and eighty-eight parts

The Méridien comes apart in the order a watchmaker lifts it: crystal, bezel, hands, dial, then calibre A·01. Each part you see is drawn live.

Winding

Seventy-two hours in reserve

Forty turns of the crown pass through two steel wheels and coil the mainspring in its barrel. It releases its force over three days.

Going train

Four wheels, at their true speed

Tooth into leaf, four gilt wheels gear the barrel's slow pull upward until the last completes one turn a minute. Nothing here is hurried for the camera.

Escapement

Six beats to the second

The balance swings six times a second beneath its bridge. Each swing lets the escape wheel advance by half a tooth, and that small release is the tick.

Savoir-faire

Finished by hand, on both sides

Turn the Méridien over and the calibre is in plain view: striped bridges, blued screws, jewels set in gold. The side nobody sees is finished to the same standard as the dial.
